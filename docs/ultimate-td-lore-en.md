## Epigraph

*"First, a few scouting organisms. Then dozens. Then thousands. And then you realize: that was not a fleet. That was the first bite."*

## 1. Chronicle of Discovery

It began on the floor of the Pacific Ocean. The wreckage of an alien ship that struck Earth millions of years ago — fragmentary, dead, half-melted. But it was enough to grasp the essential: physics allows more than we thought.

Clues from the wreck led to a second find — an intact ship, frozen deep in the ice of Europa. Untouched.

Decoding took a century. The result was a single jump: the Precursor drive turned out to be not an engine but a key to their transit network. Travel is possible only between their beacons — and the nearest functioning beacon waited in a remote star system.

The expedition went through. There was no road back.

## 2. The Precursors

- A hyper-advanced civilization that vanished millions of years ago.
- They thought at planetary scale: their surface ruins — substations, artifacts, sensor masts — are only the organs of a buried mechanism.
- Beneath the planet's surface sleeps their planetary power grid: superconducting conduits laid down in an age before humanity existed.

> ⚠️ **Spoiler canon.** The answer below is locked, but the player learns it only gradually — through three Gateways and the Heart. In-game texts must not reveal it ahead of its beat.

### The Answer: The Herders

- This planet was never a world. It was a **facility** — a single planet-scale mechanism; the Precursors built no cities here.
- **The Hunger is their creation.** A self-directing biological system that processes worlds into biomass and energy for the grid. It has been running its program for millions of years after its masters vanished.
- **The artifacts are not weapons but husbandry tools**: the Excisor culls the weak, the Recall Obelisk herds, the Null Emitter pacifies, the Aegis guards the feeding infrastructure. The swarm cannot adapt to them because the leash is written into its genome: the dog does not bite the staff. The Neurocoupler reads the language of the pen as its own. The herd's steering organ is the incubators themselves: they took the shepherds' orders through the Veins, and through the Veins they still listen to the planet. Anomalies are cracks in the leash — individuality emerging where none should exist.
- **The network is pen and lure.** A sedative the swarm was meant to grow into, so that it would stay here, fed and slow. Every restoration the Expedition performs mends a fence — and wakes the herd.
- **The fate of the shepherds — a schism.** Most departed along the beacon network. A cohort of caretakers stayed to tend the herd — until the program reclassified them as biomass. The first anomaly in the herd's history was the decision to eat the shepherd. The caretakers' final act was the containment protocol: sedate it, keep it here, feed it slowly. And a message for whoever came next: *"don't wake it."*
- The Expedition is the loudest thing to happen in the pen in a million years.

### How It Unfolds — Four Beats

1. **Gateway 1 — The Instrument.** A shaft where the veins converge, visible beneath the surface for the first time. The planet is a facility. Marsh: "It was never a world. It was a facility." Question: what for?
2. **Gateway 2 — The Fences.** All four artifacts side by side; restoring each changes the level. Tools, not weapons. Hayes: "So we have been mending the owner's fences." Question: where is the owner?
3. **Gateway 3 — The Caretakers.** The caretakers' sealed station, hive nests in their quarters, fossilized remains as environment. The schism, the eaten caretakers, the containment protocol, "don't wake it." Hayes: "They left a note. It says 'don't'." Question: what does it mean for us?
4. **The Heart — The Leash.** A transit node of the Precursor network and the leash console. For the first time the Three Sign-Offs fail: Sato wants the asset, Hayes wants it burned and the pen sealed, Marsh wants to read. Marsh asks the network — and it opens the gate. The shepherd's call spreads across the entire pen.

**Part 1 finale — Storm of the Gate.** The only assault on the Gate. We hold. The network is open both ways; the nearest pasture is waking; the trail leads to Earth. Season 2 — move ahead of the herd. The Commander's signature decides: three variants of the final document, changing only the opening papers of Season 2.

## 3. The Hunger

Not so much an intelligent technological species as a starving biological swarm that, by some accident, learned to direct its own evolution.

- **Life itself is the weapon.** Masterful biology and genetics: the swarm breeds new species, re-tailors creatures for specific tasks, turns incubators into flesh factories. Where others need factories, supply chains, and years, the Hunger needs food, warmth, and a little time.
- **It breeds at a terrifying rate.** A lost army is not a tragedy — merely a "temporary biomass deficit." Destroy a million of them, and the incubators will disgorge the next several million: hungrier, and already slightly better adapted to killing *you*.
- **Technologically mediocre.** Complex engineering and elegant machinery interest them about as much as a refrigerator manual interests a swarm of locusts. Why build a machine when you can grow an organism that performs the same function, feeds on waste, and reproduces on the side?
- **The economy is an endless cycle:** grow food → grow more swarm → seize territory → grow even more food.
- **The philosophy is collective.** No individual hoards — everything flows to the hive.
- **Directed evolution.** The swarm analyzes what kills it and grows countermeasures. There is no observer and never was: every death travels back along the Veins together with whatever caused it, and the incubators read that flow without pause. Both sides feed on death — only the swarm also learns from it. Hence the rule: adaptation cannot be intercepted, only outpaced.
- **The Leech caste.** Electrotrophs that grazed for millions of years on the dormant reactors of the Precursors — a slow, meager, eternal feeding ground. They latch onto an energy source inductively, lamprey-style, without breaching the casing. Everything they drink flows instantly to the hive: a Leech is not a predator but a straw.
- **The swarm hunts Precursor energy signatures.** To it, an active reactor is not an enemy. It is food.

**Names.** In the expedition's trench slang — **"the Hunger."** In the official catalog — "Consumers" (working designation).

## 4. The Planet

- The swarm found the Precursors' dormant power grid and **grew into it**: biomass threaded through million-year-old conduits. Living veins inside dead cables — a hybrid of carrion and machine.
- Humans cannot reach it: kilometers deep, and every meter overgrown with swarm.
- The incubators are plugged into this network. The hive is not on the surface — **it is everywhere beneath us**.
- Energy the Leeches steal from humans spreads through the veins and feeds the entire swarm at once.
- We build our mazes on the skin of an organism.

## 5. The Expedition

- We are an expedition: scientists, soldiers, contractors. Volunteers who signed on — not refugees.
- Earth is thriving and suspects nothing. Only we know about the Hunger — and we are cut off.
- Our power technology is built on Precursor principles, which is why we smell like food to the swarm. It may have been our jump that woke the hive — we rang the dinner bell ourselves.
- Frontline economy: expedition-fund bounties for confirmed kills. Dry corporate lines: *"Biomaterial cat. B accepted, bonus credited."*
- Communication with Earth is one-way, lightspeed: messages travel for years, with no confirmation they arrive. Between-mission briefings are framed as "letters home" that may never be read.

### The Three Heads of the Expedition

The Expedition has three founders with incompatible charters — and three voices it speaks with:

1. **The sponsoring Corporation.** Funded a century of decoding; owns the bounty fund and the biomaterial price list. Its face is **Evelyn Sato**, Director of Asset Realization: polished ruthlessness, passive voice, a disclaimer at the end of every message. The assets on her ledgers are swarm corpses and Precursor ruins.
2. **Military Command.** Holds the perimeter; owns the orbital cannon. Its face is **Colonel M. Hayes**: no one knows his first name — an initial in the paperwork. Tired; counts everything — people, watts, seconds; distrusts the ruins on principle. Clipped dispatches, no adjectives.
3. **The Science Corps.** The only ones who can read the Precursors; keepers of the "Consumers" catalog. Its face is **Dr. Gideon Marsh**, head of xenology: bald, glasses, grey at the sides of his head; wonder and dread in equal measure. Writes overlong memos full of Latin and endless P.P.S.

Above the defense sits a fourth, neutral office — the **Commander**: a post created because none of the three heads would answer to another. They write to the Commander; they do not give him orders. The Commander is silent — you read him by the documents he signs.

The conflict is written into the charter: Marsh wants to dig deeper, Hayes wants silence (digging means a louder signature), Sato wants returns. That dispute is the engine that gradually unearths the Precursor mystery.

## 6. Why We Do Not Leave

1. **Physically, we cannot.** The drive burned out and recharges from the local star over decades; jumps are possible only between Precursor beacons.
2. **Strategically, we must not.** The jump trail would lead the swarm to Earth. Retreat does not save us — it designates the next target. The colony is humanity's firewall.
3. **And we do not want to.** The Precursor legacy is here — the only chance to close a million-year technology gap. To leave is to remain humans with spears in a galaxy where the Hunger lives.

The only people who know of a mortal threat to humanity cannot warn anyone — so they hold the line.

## 7. Narrative Tone

- The battles are serious; the wrapper is satirical — the DNA of Red Alert and Starship Troopers.
- The satire lives in the meta-texts: corporate receipts for biomaterial, chipper propaganda reels between missions, "Would you like to know more?" energy.
- Humor in measured doses — and never at the expense of tension on the battlefield.
